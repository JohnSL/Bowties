Signaling facilities — Mast, Plant, and Automatic Block Signals

A clean storyboard for the facility-first direction: add a named facility using the existing entry point, show Bowties the railroad, and let Bowties derive the repeated signal relationships.

Current direction
Selected UX direction as of 2026-10-31: Interlocking Plant and Automatic Block Signals (renamed from Signal Plant and Signal Line in November 2026). Add Facility no longer offers Signal Mast separately. Masts remain the individual signals configured within an interlocking or automatic block territory; the Mast walkthrough at the end is kept as configuration reference. The dividing test: if any signal tells a train which route it will take, it belongs to a Plant; otherwise it is an automatic block signal, even with sidings and hand-thrown turnouts. Detailed workflow design and persistent facility ownership remain open.

Mockup scope (not product UI)

  • No hardware access or application occurs. Occupancy states, indication tables, Rulebook entries, and lamp assignments are illustrative, not validated mappings.
  • Selected controls preview choices, not a synchronized end-to-end plan. Automatic Block Signals' later signal counts, links, and connection tables retain the baseline example.
  • Both directions: Appearance simulates opposing signals and APB direction as expected aspects only. Configuring opposing signals, two-ended external connections, and validated bidirectional/APB logic are not implemented.

Design notes — not product UI

Behavior

  • One block is sufficient. Select existing named, detected blocks without renaming or rebinding them; missing detection is an incomplete prerequisite, not configured here.
  • Removing membership does not delete a shared block or detector. Reordering must preserve signal identities and bindings while previewing changed downstream references.
  • Direction count and coordination are separate. Both directions does not imply APB, and neither coordination policy is selected automatically.
  • Occupancy observations can update independently of draft edits; unavailable observations are not current state.
  • Blocks and turnouts share one list in track order, because that is how modelers describe their layout. Signals are derived from the list and appear only in the diagram, so nobody tries to move or delete a signal in the list.
  • Every turnout belongs to one block: the block that detects a car standing on its points. Its position feeds that block's entrance signals, one per signaled direction, as the prototype switch circuit controller does. Where it sits inside the block does not matter to the signals, only to the drawing. A turnout lined for the spur keeps the block's signals at Stop even after the train has cleared into the spur. The signal before drops to Approach.
  • Turnouts need no signals of their own. Mid-block turnouts, such as industry spurs, are the common prototype case; a turnout at a gap is simply the first or last turnout of a block.
  • Moving a block moves its turnouts. Up and down step a turnout within its block and on into the neighbouring block.
  • The list is in the order a train passes the blocks, in the direction of travel. Changing the direction never reorders the list; the diagram redraws with the first row at the other end, and the signals, their names, and the wording follow. Blocks entered with the wrong direction set are fixed by picking the other direction. With both directions, the same choice says which way the list is written.
  • The block name and occupancy dot sit at the centre of each block; turnouts are drawn clear of the centre. Selecting a block or turnout shades its signal block, including any joined blocks; the signals at the ends of the shading protect everything inside it.
  • Joining a block to the one before it (no signals at that boundary) is uncommon, so it is possible rather than prominent: "Remove the signal between …" in the block's "⋯" row menu, with a note on when to use it, such as a turnout detected on its own. A joined row says "No signal between it and …"; other rows add no text. The diagram keeps a dashed "ghost" signal at the boundary; clicking it, or "Add a signal between …" in the same menu, turns the signals back on. An earlier checkbox on every row was too easy to toggle while selecting the row.
  • The list scrolls; "Add block…" and "Add turnout in block" stay in a strip below it. A new turnout goes in the selected block, or after the selected turnout in its block. Longer guidance sits behind the "?" on the list heading.
  • Points end and diverging side are drawing-only. The selected turnout's shape is chosen from four pictures in the diagram header, which adds no height and never covers the drawing. A pair of turnouts whose diverging legs meet draws as a siding.
  • Signals protect blocks, not turnouts. One entrance signal per direction covers every turnout inside a block, as with a prototype siding inside one block. Signals right at the siding ends come from splitting detection so the turnouts fall at block boundaries.
  • The prototype uses a switch circuit controller that makes the block look occupied; Bowties keeps the turnout as a distinct reason so Review and troubleshooting can name it.
  • No signal is generated for the siding exit. A switch indicator for crews leaving the siding could later be a Block Indicator that reads the main-track blocks.

Mockup limitations

  • Block add, remove, and reorder are illustrative; the three sample blocks stay fixed. Turnout add, remove, move, and shape choice work. Joining blocks updates only this page's diagram.
  • A Plant can connect at either end, set from a chip on the grey approach track ("+ Plant", or the Plant's name). At the far end our last signal reads the Plant's entrance signal; at the near end the Plant's exit signal reads our first signal. The far end can instead be "nothing beyond" or "nothing beyond, treat as Stop" for staging. The chip's menu holds those choices; "Choose a Plant…" opens a searchable Plant selector. Chips are editable only here; later pages show the result as a label.
  • This diagram and the signal pickers on later pages are one shared track drawing. On later pages, turnouts are shown but not editable, and selecting a signal configures it.

Open questions

  • Does the one-block path remain simple, including facility versus signal naming?
  • Where does a turnout's position come from? Points feedback, the last command sent, or either, and how is that chosen when adding a turnout to the list? The mockup's "Add turnout" creates a placeholder with no source.
  • Should an optional fouling or siding-occupancy input join the turnout position?
  • Bidirectional membership, opposite-end entrances, and section-level APB/CTC coordination need design without duplicating block identities or detection.

Design notes — not product UI

Behavior

  • Protected-block and internal downstream references derive from order. Only the final signal needs an external downstream reference.
  • Symbols show configured direction and protection, not live aspects or physical installation. Direction changes preserve names and bindings. Occupancy is separate; offline never presents retained observations as current.
  • Aspect count replaces signaling family and vocabulary. 2 aspects uses occupancy only; 3 adds Approach from the next signal (the default); 4 adds Advance Approach two signals before a Stop, which is uncommon on layouts. Changing the setting for all signals keeps individual overrides.
  • A signal with no downstream signal shows Stop / Clear whatever the count. Clear does not claim that an unmonitored path beyond the block is clear.
  • What lies beyond the last block is set from its chip on Blocks: nothing (Stop / Clear), nothing but treated as Stop (Approach at best, for staging or the end of signaled track), or a Plant entrance chosen in the Plant selector. Here it is shown read-only, on the diagram and under the rules.
  • Automatic block signals show a small, nearly universal set: Clear, Approach, and Stop. Speed versus route signaling, rulebook vocabularies, and diverging indications belong to Interlocking Plants, which govern routes.
  • The rules table is a meaning preview, not live indications or authority. Appearance maps these meanings to signal types and lets the modeler try them; a head never chooses the meaning.
  • Longer explanations sit behind small "?" help bubbles so the settings stay compact. A bubble closes on a click outside it or Escape.
  • A reversed or unknown turnout adds a distinct Stop or Restricting condition to each affected signal; it never produces a diverging indication.
  • The endpoint opens existing connection controls without creating a Plant or signal. A Plant entrance remains Plant-owned. By default its restrictions fold into Approach here.

Open questions

  • Should the last signal before a Plant optionally repeat the home signal's route (Approach Diverging / Approach Medium)? That is the one place interlocking vocabulary reaches automatic signals; the Plant would supply it.
  • Unavailable-input behavior needs validation. An unresolved reference must not imply Clear.

Design notes — not product UI

Why Appearance is its own page

  • Appearance answers what the modeler will see; Wiring answers which Signal LCC pins drive it. They are often decided at different times. Appearance hands Wiring the lamps each head needs and which lamps each aspect lights; Wiring turns that into the Rule to Aspect table.
  • Signal type is one choice for the whole facility, with a per-signal exception, matching the all-signals / selected-signal pattern on Aspects. Most ABS lines use one type throughout.
  • One head by default: Clear, Approach, Advance Approach, Stop, and flashing-red Restricting fit on one head for every type shown. A second head appears only when it is needed: Red over lunar adds a one-lamp lunar head below the signals that protect a turnout. It is its own small head with one output, not a copy of the main head. Other multi-head masts are mostly an Interlocking Plant concern.

Two kinds of trying it

  • Live follows real block detectors and turnout positions. For ABS the aspects follow directly from occupancy.
  • Live, APB: occupancy alone is not enough. Which way traffic is established depends on which end a train entered, which is history. Bowties must have seen the train enter, or read the direction state from configured hardware; until then opposing signals show Unknown, never a guess.
  • Simulated train places a train with a direction and steps it block by block, so APB direction comes with the train. Clicking a block adds or removes standing cars (occupancy with no direction); clicking a turnout lines it for the main or the siding.
  • Both kinds are expectations computed from these settings, never observations of the signals. Simulated occupancy is drawn differently from live readings, and nothing is sent to the bus.

Small signals on the track

  • The diagram shows one simple mark per signal: color for the aspect, blinking for flashing, an empty dark lamp for approach-lit Dark, and a dashed ? for unknown. It reads at any size and does not depend on signal type.
  • The selected signal's mast is drawn beside the track in its real type, with no text; its name, aspect, and reason are in the tooltip. "All masts in track order" shows the realistic sequence a crew would see.
  • Head shapes: searchlight is a round black target around one colored lens; color light is a black capsule; position and color position lights sit on a round black target.

Mockup limitations

  • Opposite-direction signals use the settings for all signals; per-signal settings for them are not designed yet.
  • A Plant at either end is assumed to show Stop. Joined blocks are not reflected here. Live APB uses the static sample, so the traffic direction is never observed.
  • Appearances are illustrative, not rulebook-certified, and catalog and hardware compatibility still require validation. Restricting is drawn as flashing red or red over lunar, and Advance Approach as flashing Approach; position light has no Advance Approach mapping here.

Design notes — not product UI

One Signal LCC per signal

  • The node is chosen first because it is where the signal runs: its mast (Rule to Aspect), its conditionals, and the track circuit receiver for the next signal all live there. Every head on the signal, including a lunar head, uses that node.
  • Only Signal LCC nodes are offered; the Signal LCC 32H is left out because "Linked to Previous" chains heads there, not rules. This page is Signal LCC–specific by design, not profile driven.
  • Bowties picks the mast slot; it is shown for traceability, not chosen. The usage line counts pins, conditionals, and masts on the node. Conditionals run out first, so they are shown as an estimate (about one per rule) until the logic is generated.
  • The node is shown in a field-style button (name, location, node ID, "Change ▾"); clicking anywhere on it opens a search dialog that matches name, description, and node ID, and by default hides nodes that are offline, have no free mast, or lack enough free pins.

Lamps and pins work like a seat map

  • Lamps are the passengers and pins are the seats. Select a lamp, then click a pin: a free pin places it and selection moves to the next lamp without a pin; the selected lamp's own pin clears it; another lamp's pin on this signal swaps the two. Pins used by other signals or facilities are disabled and show the facility's name.
  • The selected lamp's pin is filled in the grid, and every pin this signal uses shows its lamp name, so the mapping reads both ways without a second control.
  • Header labels (H1–H4) place every lamp of the selected lamp's head on that header, matching lamps to the G, Y, R, L pin labels, which wires most signals in one click. The labels are only a suggestion: any pin can drive any LED, so the user can place lamps on any pins, and Blink confirms the result.
  • Signal type comes from Appearance; Wiring only chooses how each head is driven. Two choices are a segmented control and one is plain text; the electrical detail is a tooltip.

Pins used outside Bowties

  • A Signal LCC can already drive pins from configuration Bowties does not manage: a mast or Direct Lamp row set up by hand, by another tool, or by earlier testing. Each such pin shows its owner ("Mast 3", "Direct lamp 4") with a dashed outline, so it is never just "Taken".
  • Clicking one opens its details in place: the owner, its description, every pin it drives and, for a mast, its rules and the Logic conditionals that set its aspects. Those are found by event ID: a conditional whose action sends one of the mast's set-aspect events drives that mast.
  • The card closes with the × in its corner (named "Close Mast 3 details", since the editor has its own close), by clicking the pin again, or with Escape, which closes only the card. Focus returns to the pin. In E2c's confirmation, Cancel goes back to the details and × closes the card.
  • Show in Configuration is a button, not a link, because it leaves a dialog. The editor is not closed or saved: its choices stay, and returning to the Railroad tab shows it again where it was. The status line says so when the user leaves. The Railroad panel also links to the same configuration without an open dialog (see After Done).
  • Release and use frees every pin of that owner, not just the one clicked, and places the selected lamp on the clicked pin. A released mast is reset together with the conditionals that set its aspects, since they exist only to drive it; a conditional whose actions also send events something else consumes is kept and named in the confirmation. The resets are staged with the facility's own edits: they show as unsaved changes in the node's configuration, and Discard brings them back. Pins used by a Bowties facility cannot be released here.
  • Built in Spec 020 E2c, after facility removal (E2b), which supplies the reset edits. Until then the details show and Release and use is disabled. Importing such configuration as a facility is a separate idea (specs/ideas/features/import-existing-signal-configuration.md).

Lamp patterns stay closed

  • The Rule to Aspect table is derived from Appearance and the pins, so it starts closed. It shows what will be written to the mast: one row per Signal LCC rule, one column per pin, each cell a lamp in that rule's Appearance list with its phase. Flashing lamps share phase A so a blended yellow flashes as one.
  • Customize lamps is the escape hatch for unusual signals: edits are outlined, a rule lights at most 4 lamps, and Reset to Appearance discards them. The Show check lives here because it works per rule.

Checking the wiring

  • Blink proves one pin is wired to the right lamp. It briefly uses Direct Lamp Control, so Bowties must always return that lamp to Used by Mast afterwards; a lamp left in direct mode silently breaks every aspect.
  • Show proves a whole aspect by sending the rule's set aspect event, the same staged order as the community bring-up procedure. Both need the node online and the signal fully wired. When nothing lights, the hint points at the board's CA/CC jumper, which sets whether the outputs pull up or pull down to light an LED, to match common-anode or common-cathode wiring.

Mockup limitations

  • Nodes, pins, and usage outside this facility are sample data. Blink and Show send nothing.
  • When a head has no pins yet for its signal type, drive, or node, Bowties keeps it on the same header if that header is free, and otherwise uses the first free one. Earlier pins are kept so switching back restores them; removed heads release their pins.
  • With both directions, only the chain for the list direction is wired here. Opposite-direction signals and coordination resources are not allocated.

Open questions

  • Blink: does changing a lamp to Direct Lamp Control take effect without restarting the node? When the lamp returns to Used by Mast, does it show the current aspect at once or only at the next aspect change?
  • How many conditionals does a signal really use? The usage line is an estimate until Bowties generates the logic.

    Behavior

    • Review differences against the existing configuration, including membership, resource releases, and changed links. Editing must not recreate unchanged signal identities.
    • All five Cancel buttons are preview-only: nothing is saved or applied, local choices remain until reload. Real draft cancellation/persistence is not implemented here.

    Mockup limitations

    • Save/apply controls are disabled because authoritative checks remain pending. Edit mode compares against the initial sample, not a loaded layout; change counts include effective settings, relationships, and active bindings.
    • The checks here are limited sample checks.     4. How the three facility scales connect

    Ownership remains singular at the boundaries. The last automatic signal may read a Plant entrance as its downstream signal, but it does not become part of two facilities.

    Ownership rule

    Signal Mast owns

    One combined indication and its physical display.
    • Protected block
    • Next-signal cascade
    • Heads, appearances, and outputs

    Interlocking Plant owns

    Signals that govern routes at one location.
    • Home and siding departure signals
    • Routes, conflicts, diverging indications
    • Power switch bindings
    • Speed or route vocabulary

    Automatic Block Signals may own

    The ordered signals along a stretch of main track.
    • Block sequence
    • Generated Mast chain and aspect count
    • Hand-thrown turnout restrictions
    • APB direction coordination

    The storyboard is useful before every domain name and persistence rule is settled. These are the specific conclusions a review should confirm or overturn.
