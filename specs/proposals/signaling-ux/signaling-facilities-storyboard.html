Signaling facilities — Mast, Plant, and Automatic Block Signals

A clean storyboard for the facility-first direction: add a named facility using the existing entry point, show Bowties the railroad, and let Bowties derive the repeated signal relationships.

Current direction
Selected UX direction as of 2026-10-31: Interlocking Plant and Automatic Block Signals (renamed from Signal Plant and Signal Line in November 2026). Add Facility no longer offers Signal Mast separately. Masts remain the individual signals configured within an interlocking or automatic block territory; the Mast walkthrough at the end is kept as configuration reference. The dividing test: if any signal tells a train which route it will take, it belongs to a Plant; otherwise it is an automatic block signal, even with sidings and hand-thrown turnouts. Detailed workflow design and persistent facility ownership remain open.

Mockup scope (not product UI)

  • Both directions records operating intent only. Opposing signal views, two-ended external connections, and bidirectional/APB logic are not implemented.

Design notes — not product UI

Behavior

  • One block is sufficient. Select existing named, detected blocks without renaming or rebinding them; missing detection is an incomplete prerequisite, not configured here.
  • Removing membership does not delete a shared block or detector. Reordering must preserve signal identities and bindings while previewing changed downstream references.
  • Direction count and coordination are separate. Both directions does not imply APB, and neither coordination policy is selected automatically.
  • Occupancy observations can update independently of draft edits; unavailable observations are not current state.
  • Blocks and turnouts share one list in track order, because that is how modelers describe their layout. Signals are derived from the list and appear only in the diagram, so nobody tries to move or delete a signal in the list.
  • Every turnout belongs to one block: the block that detects a car standing on its points. Its position feeds that block's entrance signals, one per signaled direction, as the prototype switch circuit controller does. Where it sits inside the block does not matter to the signals, only to the drawing. A turnout lined for the spur keeps the block's signals at Stop even after the train has cleared into the spur. The signal before drops to Approach.
  • Turnouts need no signals of their own. Mid-block turnouts, such as industry spurs, are the common prototype case; a turnout at a gap is simply the first or last turnout of a block.
  • Moving a block moves its turnouts. Up and down step a turnout within its block and on into the neighbouring block.
  • The list is in the order a train passes the blocks, in the direction of travel. Changing the direction never reorders the list; the diagram redraws with the first row at the other end, and the signals, their names, and the wording follow. Blocks entered with the wrong direction set are fixed by picking the other direction. With both directions, the same choice says which way the list is written.
  • The block name and occupancy dot sit at the centre of each block; turnouts are drawn clear of the centre. Selecting a block or turnout shades its signal block, including any joined blocks; the signals at the ends of the shading protect everything inside it.
  • Joining a block to the one before it (no signals at that boundary) is uncommon, so it is possible rather than prominent: "Remove the signal between …" in the block's "⋯" row menu, with a note on when to use it, such as a turnout detected on its own. A joined row says "No signal between it and …"; other rows add no text. The diagram keeps a dashed "ghost" signal at the boundary; clicking it, or "Add a signal between …" in the same menu, turns the signals back on. An earlier checkbox on every row was too easy to toggle while selecting the row.
  • The list scrolls; "Add block…" and "Add turnout in block" stay in a strip below it. A new turnout goes in the selected block, or after the selected turnout in its block. Longer guidance sits behind the "?" on the list heading.
  • Points end and diverging side are drawing-only. The selected turnout's shape is chosen from four pictures in the diagram header, which adds no height and never covers the drawing. A pair of turnouts whose diverging legs meet draws as a siding.
  • Signals protect blocks, not turnouts. One entrance signal per direction covers every turnout inside a block, as with a prototype siding inside one block. Signals right at the siding ends come from splitting detection so the turnouts fall at block boundaries.
  • The prototype uses a switch circuit controller that makes the block look occupied; Bowties keeps the turnout as a distinct reason so Review and troubleshooting can name it.
  • No signal is generated for the siding exit. A switch indicator for crews leaving the siding could later be a Block Indicator that reads the main-track blocks.

Mockup limitations

  • Block add, remove, and reorder are illustrative; the three sample blocks stay fixed. Turnout add, remove, move, and shape choice work. Joining blocks updates only this page's diagram.
  • A Plant can connect at either end, set from a chip on the grey approach track ("+ Plant", or the Plant's name). At the far end our last signal reads the Plant's entrance signal; at the near end the Plant's exit signal reads our first signal. The far end can instead be "nothing beyond" or "nothing beyond, treat as Stop" for staging. The chip's menu holds those choices; "Choose a Plant…" opens a searchable Plant selector. Chips are editable only here; later pages show the result as a label.
  • This diagram and the signal pickers on later pages are one shared track drawing. On later pages, turnouts are shown but not editable, and selecting a signal configures it.

Open questions

  • Does the one-block path remain simple, including facility versus signal naming?
  • Where does a turnout's position come from? Points feedback, the last command sent, or either, and how is that chosen when adding a turnout to the list? The mockup's "Add turnout" creates a placeholder with no source.
  • Should an optional fouling or siding-occupancy input join the turnout position?
  • Bidirectional membership, opposite-end entrances, and section-level APB/CTC coordination need design without duplicating block identities or detection.

Design notes — not product UI

Behavior

  • Protected-block and internal downstream references derive from order. Only the final signal needs an external downstream reference.
  • Symbols show configured direction and protection, not live aspects or physical installation. Direction changes preserve names and bindings. Occupancy is separate; offline never presents retained observations as current.
  • Aspect count replaces signaling family and vocabulary. 2 aspects uses occupancy only; 3 adds Approach from the next signal (the default); 4 adds Advance Approach two signals before a Stop, which is uncommon on layouts. Changing the setting for all signals keeps individual overrides.
  • A signal with no downstream signal shows Stop / Clear whatever the count. Clear does not claim that an unmonitored path beyond the block is clear.
  • What lies beyond the last block is set from its chip on Blocks: nothing (Stop / Clear), nothing but treated as Stop (Approach at best, for staging or the end of signaled track), or a Plant entrance chosen in the Plant selector. Here it is shown read-only, on the diagram and under the rules.
  • Automatic block signals show a small, nearly universal set: Clear, Approach, and Stop. Speed versus route signaling, rulebook vocabularies, and diverging indications belong to Interlocking Plants, which govern routes.
  • The rules table is a meaning preview, not live indications or authority. Physical signals maps these meanings to appearances separately; a head never chooses the meaning.
  • Longer explanations sit behind small "?" help bubbles so the settings stay compact. A bubble closes on a click outside it or Escape.
  • A reversed or unknown turnout adds a distinct Stop or Restricting condition to each affected signal; it never produces a diverging indication.
  • The endpoint opens existing connection controls without creating a Plant or signal. A Plant entrance remains Plant-owned. By default its restrictions fold into Approach here.

Open questions

  • Should the last signal before a Plant optionally repeat the home signal's route (Approach Diverging / Approach Medium)? That is the one place interlocking vocabulary reaches automatic signals; the Plant would supply it.
  • Unavailable-input behavior needs validation. An unresolved reference must not imply Clear.

Design notes — not product UI

  • Aspects and Physical signals share the track drawing from Blocks. Signal selection, head selection, names, policies, and bindings survive navigation in this session.
  • Future UX: sample hardware and illustrative appearances only, not current S2 support. Choose the board physically near this signal, not whichever board has free outputs; the mockup never switches boards.
  • The board line is a mock resource check over sample boards, not an allocator or live availability, and the board stays selected if outputs run out. Mast mappings are examples, not rulebook-certified; advanced mapping is future design.
  • With both directions, only the chain for the list direction is shown. Opposite-direction signals and coordination resources are not allocated.
  • Head appearance is independent of electrical implementation: RG searchlight blending uses two outputs; RYG searchlight uses three. Fixed RYG lamps use three. Position-light groups plus shared center use four, or three with explicitly selected external center-light circuitry.
  • Center is lit for every lit position-light indication and dark when the head darkens. Output counts are independently controlled board outputs, not total physical wires.
  • Sample checks cover active outputs across all heads/signals, scoped by node, and illustrative capacity. Existing external claims and actual hardware remain unvalidated. No auto-allocation or automatic board switching.
  • Changing appearance/implementation/board retains previous binding variants for restoration; removed heads release their claims. Plant reuse is a future editor seam, not a change to the Plant pages.

    4. How the three facility scales connect

    Ownership remains singular at the boundaries. The last automatic signal may read a Plant entrance as its downstream signal, but it does not become part of two facilities.
